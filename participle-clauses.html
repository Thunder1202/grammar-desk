Participle clauses

General info

A participle clause is a short clause that begins with an -ing or -ed form (or having + -ed) instead of a full clause with a subject and a verb. It joins two ideas in fewer words and sounds smooth and written.

Full clause and participle clause

While she was walking home, she met an old friend. → Walking home, she met an old friend.

Because he was tired, he went to bed. → Tired, he went to bed.

After she had finished her work, she left. → Having finished her work, she left.

We use them to say

  • Time: Opening the door, he saw a cat.
  • Reason: Not wanting to be late, she took a taxi.
  • Result: The factory closed, putting 200 people out of work.
  • Condition or contrast: Used properly, the tool lasts for years.
  • Description: The man standing at the door is my uncle.
Russian has the деепричастие (идя, сделав) and Uzbek has forms like -ib and -gach (uyga borib, ishni tugatgach). English participle clauses do the same job, but one -ing form can mean time, reason or result, so the context decides.

Причастный оборот (participle clause) — это короткая часть предложения, которая начинается с формы -ing или -ed (или having + -ed) вместо полного придаточного с подлежащим и глаголом. Он соединяет две мысли меньшим числом слов и звучит гладко и письменно.

Полное придаточное и оборот

While she was walking home, she met an old friend. → Walking home, she met an old friend.

Because he was tired, he went to bed. → Tired, he went to bed.

After she had finished her work, she left. → Having finished her work, she left.

Что можно выразить

  • Время: Opening the door, he saw a cat.
  • Причину: Not wanting to be late, she took a taxi.
  • Результат: The factory closed, putting 200 people out of work.
  • Условие или противопоставление: Used properly, the tool lasts for years.
  • Описание: The man standing at the door is my uncle.
В русском есть деепричастие (идя, сделав), в узбекском — формы -ib и -gach (uyga borib, ishni tugatgach). Английские обороты делают то же самое, но одна форма -ing может значить время, причину или результат, поэтому смысл подсказывает контекст.

Sifatdosh/ravishdosh gapi (participle clause) — ega va fe'li bor to'liq gap o'rniga -ing yoki -ed shakli (yoki having + -ed) bilan boshlanadigan qisqa gap bo'lagi. U ikki fikrni kamroq so'z bilan bog'laydi va silliq, yozma eshitiladi.

  1. Same subject: the doer of the participle is the subject of the main clause. Walking home, she met a friend (she was walking). Walking home, the rain started is wrong, because the rain was not walking.
  2. Active or passive: -ing means the subject does the action. -ed (or being + -ed) means the subject receives it: Shocked by the news, she sat down.
  3. Time order: a simple -ing clause is at the same time as, or just before, the main action: Opening the door, he saw a cat. Use having + -ed for a clearly earlier action: Having eaten, we left.
  4. States: being, feeling, knowing, wanting also work: Knowing the city well, she gave good advice.
  5. Negative: put not before the participle: Not knowing the answer, he guessed. (not Knowing not ...)
  6. Commas: use a comma after a participle clause at the start. In the middle or at the end it is separated by commas, and a result -ing clause follows a comma: ..., causing a leak.
  7. Conjunctions for clarity: while, when, after, before, once, since, although and on can come before -ing or -ed: Before leaving, ... On arriving, ... Once cooked, ...
  8. Register: participle clauses are formal and written. In speech use full clauses, and do not stack several in one sentence.
  1. Одно подлежащее: тот, кто делает действие в обороте, — это подлежащее главной части. Walking home, she met a friend (она шла). Walking home, the rain started — ошибка, потому что дождь не шёл домой.
  2. Действие или пассив: -ing значит, что подлежащее само делает действие. -ed (или being + -ed) значит, что оно получает действие: Shocked by the news, she sat down.
  3. Порядок во времени: простой оборот с -ing — одновременно с главным действием или сразу перед ним: Opening the door, he saw a cat. Для явно более раннего действия — having + -ed: Having eaten, we left.
  4. Состояния: being, feeling, knowing, wanting тоже подходят: Knowing the city well, she gave good advice.
  5. Отрицание: not ставим перед причастием: Not knowing the answer, he guessed. (не Knowing not ...)
  6. Запятые: после оборота в начале ставим запятую. В середине или в конце он отделяется запятыми, а оборот результата идёт после запятой: ..., causing a leak.
  7. Союзы для ясности: while, when, after, before, once, since, although и on можно ставить перед -ing или -ed: Before leaving, ... On arriving, ... Once cooked, ...
  8. Стиль: обороты формальны и письменны. В речи используй полные предложения и не нагромождай несколько оборотов в одном предложении.
  1. Bitta ega: shakldagi ishni bajaruvchi bosh gapning egasi bo'ladi. Walking home, she met a friend (u yurgan). Walking home, the rain started xato, chunki yomg'ir uyga yurmagan.
  2. Aniq yoki majhul: -ing ega ishni o'zi qilishini bildiradi. -ed (yoki being + -ed) ega ishni qabul qilishini bildiradi: Shocked by the news, she sat down.
  3. Vaqt tartibi: oddiy -ing shakli asosiy ish bilan bir vaqtda yoki undan biroz oldin bo'ladi: Opening the door, he saw a cat. Aniq oldingi ish uchun having + -ed: Having eaten, we left.
  4. Holatlar: being, feeling, knowing, wanting ham mos: Knowing the city well, she gave good advice.
  5. Inkor: not ni shakldan oldin qo'ying: Not knowing the answer, he guessed. (Knowing not ... emas)
  6. Vergul: boshdagi shakldan keyin vergul qo'yiladi. O'rtada yoki oxirda vergul bilan ajratiladi, natija -ing si esa verguldan keyin keladi: ..., causing a leak.
  7. Aniqlik uchun bog'lovchilar: while, when, after, before, once, since, although va on -ing yoki -ed dan oldin kelishi mumkin: Before leaving, ... On arriving, ... Once cooked, ...
  8. Uslub: bunday shakllar rasmiy va yozma. Nutqda to'liq gaplar ishlating va bir gapga bir nechtasini yig'mang.

Student explanation

Ask yourself three questions

  1. Is the subject the same in both parts? Yes → you can use a participle clause. No → keep the full clause.
  2. Does the subject do the action or receive it? Does → -ing. Receives → -ed.
  3. Did the action happen earlier? Yes → having + -ed.

Build it in 3 steps

Because she felt tired, she went to bed. 1) Check the subject: she / she. Same. 2) Remove because and the subject: felt tired. 3) Change the verb to -ing: feeling tired. → Feeling tired, she went to bed. (Even shorter: Tired, she went to bed.)

Why it works

The listener knows who is doing both actions, so you do not need to say the subject twice. The sentence becomes shorter and flows better.

Common mistakes

  • Walking home, the rain started. Walking home, she got wet in the rain.
  • Finish the work, she left. Having finished the work, she left.
  • Knowing not the answer, he guessed. Not knowing the answer, he guessed.
  • Surprising by the news, he sat down. Surprised by the news, he sat down.
Check the doer first. If the two parts have different subjects, the sentence sounds wrong. Also check -ing / -ed: surprising = it surprises people, surprised = a person feels surprise.

Задай себе три вопроса

  1. Подлежащее в обеих частях одно и то же? Да → можно использовать оборот. Нет → оставь полное предложение.
  2. Подлежащее делает действие или получает его? Делает → -ing. Получает → -ed.
  3. Действие было раньше? Да → having + -ed.

Строим за 3 шага

Because she felt tired, she went to bed. 1) Проверь подлежащее: she / she. Одно и то же. 2) Убери because и подлежащее: felt tired. 3) Поставь глагол в -ing: feeling tired. → Feeling tired, she went to bed. (Ещё короче: Tired, she went to bed.)

Почему это работает

Слушатель знает, кто делает оба действия, поэтому подлежащее не нужно повторять. Предложение становится короче и течёт лучше.

Частые ошибки

  • Walking home, the rain started. Walking home, she got wet in the rain.
  • Finish the work, she left. Having finished the work, she left.
  • Knowing not the answer, he guessed. Not knowing the answer, he guessed.
  • Surprising by the news, he sat down. Surprised by the news, he sat down.
Сначала проверь, кто делает действие. Если в частях разные подлежащие, предложение звучит неправильно. Проверь и -ing / -ed: surprising = удивляет других, surprised = человек удивлён.

O'zingizga uchta savol bering

  1. Ikkala qismda ega bir xilmi? Ha → qisqa shakl ishlatish mumkin. Yo'q → to'liq gapni qoldiring.
  2. Ega ishni o'zi qiladimi yoki qabul qiladimi? Qiladi → -ing. Qabul qiladi → -ed.
  3. Ish oldin bo'lganmi? Ha → having + -ed.

Tinglovchi ikkala ishni kim qilayotganini biladi, shuning uchun egani ikki marta aytish shart emas. Gap qisqaroq va silliqroq bo'ladi.

  • Knowing not the answer, he guessed. Not knowing the answer, he guessed.

Real-life use

Participle clauses make writing shorter and more elegant. They are common in essays, reports, stories, news and formal applications.

Storytelling

"Hearing a noise, she turned around. Having locked the door, he walked away."

News and reports

"Speaking at a press conference, the minister denied the claims. Built in 1850, the bridge is now closed to traffic."

CV and cover letters

"Having worked in sales for five years, I am ready for a management role. Motivated and well organised, I enjoy new challenges."

Academic writing

"Used correctly, this method gives reliable results. Prices rose sharply, leading to a fall in demand."

IELTS Writing

"Having considered both sides, I believe the advantages outweigh the disadvantages. Technology has improved, making communication easier."

In speaking

Keep it light. A few short ones sound natural: "Having said that, ..." "Generally speaking, ..." "Judging by the weather, ..."

English only

Examples and drill

-ing clauses

Walking home, she met an old friend. Feeling hungry, he made a sandwich. The company grew fast, creating many new jobs.

-ed clauses

Shocked by the news, she sat down. Written in 1990, the book is still popular. Left alone, the children played quietly.

Perfect and passive

Having finished her work, she left. Having been warned, he took care. Being a teacher, she knows how to explain.

Negative and with conjunctions

Not knowing the way, she asked. Before leaving, check the lights. Although tired, he kept working. On arriving, call me.

After a noun

The man standing at the door is my uncle. The photos taken last year are very good. People living in cities often use public transport.
