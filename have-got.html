Have got

General info

have got talks about what people and things have: possessions, family, appearance and features. It means the same as have, and it is very common in spoken British English. It has nothing to do with "getting".

We use it for

  • Possessions: I've got a car.
  • Family and relationships: She's got two sisters.
  • Appearance: He's got brown eyes.
  • Features of things and places: The flat has got a balcony.
Uzbek says menda bor and Russian says у меня есть. English says I have got, and the short form I've got is what you hear most.

  1. Choose by person: have got with I, you, we, they. has got with he, she, it.
  2. Short forms: I've got, he's got. Here 's means has, not is.
  3. Negative: haven't got / hasn't got. With plurals and uncountables use any: I haven't got any money.
  4. Question: Have / Has comes first: Have you got a pen? Short answers do not repeat got: Yes, I have. / No, she hasn't.
  5. Only for possession: for actions use have, not have got: I have breakfast at 8. Do you have lunch at school?

Student explanation

Ask yourself three questions

  1. Is it possession or description? Yes → have got.
  2. Who is it about? I, you, we, they → have got. He, she, it → has got.
  3. Negative (sentence with not) or question? haven't / hasn't got, or Have / Has + subject + got. No do here.

Common mistakes

  • She have got a dog. She has got a dog.
  • Do you have got a pen? Have you got a pen?
  • I've got breakfast at 8. I have breakfast at 8.
  • Has she got a bike? Yes, she has got. Yes, she has.
have got is for owning and describing, not for actions. Short answers drop got.

In plain words

Have got is a simple way to say what is yours: things, family, or how you look. It means the same as have. It has nothing to do with getting anything.

  • I've got a new phone. The phone is mine.
  • She's got two sisters. These are her family.
  • He hasn't got a car. He does not own a car.

I, you, we, they: have got. He, she, it: has got.

Задай себе три вопроса

  1. Это обладание или описание? Да → have got.
  2. О ком речь? I, you, we, they → have got. He, she, it → has got.
  3. Отрицание (предложение с not) или вопрос? haven't / hasn't got, или Have / Has + подлежащее + got. Do здесь не нужен.

Частые ошибки

  • She have got a dog. She has got a dog.
  • Do you have got a pen? Have you got a pen?
  • I've got breakfast at 8. I have breakfast at 8.
  • Has she got a bike? Yes, she has got. Yes, she has.
have got — для обладания и описания, а не для действий. В кратких ответах got опускаем.

Простыми словами

Have got — простой способ сказать, что у вас есть: вещи, семья, внешность. Значит то же, что have. К слову «получать» отношения не имеет.

  • I've got a new phone. У меня новый телефон.
  • She's got two sisters. У неё две сестры.
  • He hasn't got a car. У него нет машины.

I, you, we, they: have got. He, she, it: has got.

Real-life use

People use have got to talk about family, things they have and how people look.

Your family

"I've got two brothers and a sister."

Talk about people

"She's got long black hair and brown eyes."

Ask to use things

"Have you got a pen I can borrow?" "Have you got any change?"

In shops

"Have you got this in a bigger size?"

Plans and meetings

"We've got a meeting at 10."

IELTS Speaking Part 1

"Have you got any brothers or sisters?" "Yes, I've got two sisters."
