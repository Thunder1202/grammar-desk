Present Perfect vs Past Simple

General info

Both tenses talk about the past, but they look at it differently. Past Simple is about a finished time. Present Perfect is about the past connected to now.

We use them for

  • Past Simple: a finished time, stated or clear: I saw that film last week.
  • Present Perfect: experience or result, time not stated: I have seen that film.
  • Present Perfect: a period that is not finished: I have drunk three coffees today.

Words that go with them

Past Simple: yesterday, last week, ago, in 2019, when. Present Perfect: ever, never, just, already, yet, so far, for, since, today.

Russian and Uzbek use one past form for both. In English the choice depends on the time: finished or connected to now.

Оба времени говорят о прошлом, но смотрят на него по-разному. Past Simple — про законченное время. Present Perfect — про прошлое, связанное с настоящим.

Когда используем

  • Past Simple: законченное время, названное или понятное: I saw that film last week.
  • Present Perfect: опыт или результат, время не названо: I have seen that film.
  • Present Perfect: период, который ещё не закончился: I have drunk three coffees today.

Слова-подсказки

Past Simple: yesterday, last week, ago, in 2019, when. Present Perfect: ever, never, just, already, yet, so far, for, since, today.

В русском и узбекском для обоих случаев одна форма прошедшего. В английском выбор зависит от времени: законченное или связанное с настоящим.

Ikkala zamon ham o'tmish haqida, lekin turlicha qaraydi. Past Simple — tugagan vaqt haqida. Present Perfect — hozir bilan bog'liq o'tmish haqida.

Qachon ishlatamiz

  • Past Simple: tugagan vaqt, aytilgan yoki tushunarli: I saw that film last week.
  • Present Perfect: tajriba yoki natija, vaqt aytilmagan: I have seen that film.
  • Present Perfect: hali tugamagan davr: I have drunk three coffees today.

  1. Past Simple = finished time, stated or clear: I saw her yesterday. We met in 2018. When did you arrive?
  2. Present Perfect = experience or result, no time stated: I have seen that film. I have lost my keys.
  3. Unfinished period (today, this week, this year) → Present Perfect: I have drunk three coffees today. Finished period → Past Simple: I drank three coffees yesterday.
  4. for / since with Present Perfect = a situation that continues now: I have lived here for five years. With Past Simple = it is over: I lived there for five years (and then I moved).
  5. Never use Present Perfect with a finished time (yesterday, ago, in 2019) or with when-questions. Use Past Simple: When did you arrive?
  6. Common pattern: start with Present Perfect, give details in Past Simple: I have been to Dubai. I went there in 2022 and stayed a week.

Student explanation

Ask yourself three questions

  1. Is there a finished time word? yesterday, last week, ago, in 2019, when → Past Simple.
  2. Is the time period still open? today, this week, so far, since, for (until now) → Present Perfect.
  3. Do I care about experience or result, not when? Yes → Present Perfect. Then use Past Simple for the details.

For with both tenses

I have worked here for two years. (I still work here.) / I worked there for two years. (I do not work there now.)

Common mistakes

  • I have seen him yesterday. I saw him yesterday.
  • When have you arrived? When did you arrive?
  • I lived here since 2020. I have lived here since 2020.
  • She has left two hours ago. She left two hours ago.
A finished time word is the quickest test. If you can say when, use Past Simple.

Задай себе три вопроса

  1. Есть слово законченного времени? yesterday, last week, ago, in 2019, when → Past Simple.
  2. Период ещё открыт? today, this week, so far, since, for (до сих пор) → Present Perfect.
  3. Мне важны опыт или результат, а не когда? Да → Present Perfect. Детали потом в Past Simple.

For в обоих временах

I have worked here for two years. (Всё ещё работаю.) / I worked there for two years. (Сейчас там не работаю.)

Частые ошибки

  • I have seen him yesterday. I saw him yesterday.
  • When have you arrived? When did you arrive?
  • I lived here since 2020. I have lived here since 2020.
  • She has left two hours ago. She left two hours ago.
Самая быстрая проверка — слово законченного времени. Можешь сказать когда — Past Simple.

O'zingizga uchta savol bering

  1. Tugagan vaqt so'zi bormi? yesterday, last week, ago, in 2019, when → Past Simple.
  2. Davr hali ochiqmi? today, this week, so far, since, for (hozirgacha) → Present Perfect.
  3. Menga qachon emas, tajriba yoki natija muhimmi? Ha → Present Perfect. Tafsilotlar keyin Past Simple da.

Real-life use

We switch between the two tenses all the time in interviews, news and small talk.

Job interview

"Have you worked in a restaurant before?" "Yes, I worked in a café for two years. I left last summer."

News

"The city has opened a new bridge. It opened on Monday after three years of work."

Getting to know someone

"Have you ever been to Samarkand?" "Yes, I went there with my family two years ago."

IELTS Speaking: talking about experience

"I have studied English for six years. I started when I was twelve, and I took my first test last year."

Examples and drill

Finished time → Past Simple

I saw her yesterday. We met in 2018. She left two hours ago.

Experience, no time → Present Perfect

I have seen that film. Have you ever tried sushi?

Unfinished period → Present Perfect

I have drunk three coffees today. It has rained a lot this week.

For and since

I have lived here since 2020. / I lived there for five years.

Both together

I have lost my keys. I lost them on the bus this morning.
